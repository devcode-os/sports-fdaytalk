## What the rule protects against

Roughing the passer exists to limit the ways a defender can hit the quarterback once he's in a passing motion or has just released the ball. Officials are specifically watching for hits that use excessive force, hit low, or land after the quarterback has clearly gotten rid of the ball.

<div class="callout-violation">
**Violation:** Landing on the quarterback with full body weight, even during a clean initial hit, is a foul if the defender could have avoided it.
</div>

## The specific triggers

A few hit types are called consistently:

- **Full body weight landing:** Driving the quarterback to the ground and landing on him with the defender's full weight
- **Low hits:** Forcibly hitting the quarterback at or below the knee
- **Helmet-to-helmet contact:** Hitting the quarterback's head or neck area with the helmet, forearm, or shoulder
- **Hits after the ball is gone:** Continuing into the quarterback well after he's released the pass

<div class="callout-legal">
**Legal:** A defender who is already committed to a hit before the ball is released, and who makes a clean wrap-up tackle without landing full weight or hitting the head or legs, is not penalized just because the ball came out first.
</div>

## Why it's a "judgment" penalty

Unlike a clear line like a false start, roughing the passer depends on how a hit is delivered, not just whether the quarterback was hit late. This is why nearly identical-looking hits can go either way depending on body position, timing, and whether the defender had a realistic chance to pull up. It remains one of the most argued penalties precisely because officials are judging technique and intent in real time, not measuring a fixed line.

## The penalty itself

Roughing the passer is a 15-yard penalty and an automatic first down for the offense, regardless of the down and distance before the play.
