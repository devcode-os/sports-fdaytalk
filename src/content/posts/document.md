## What is a bye week

A bye week is a scheduled week in which a team does not play a game. The NFL regular season runs 18 weeks, and each team plays 17 games, meaning every team gets exactly one week off. Coaches use the week to adjust game plans and address roster needs, while players use it to recover from the physical toll of the season.

## When bye weeks happen in the season

In the 2026 NFL season, bye weeks begin in Week 5 and continue through Week 14, with no team on a bye during Week 12 because of the Thanksgiving slate. That window has held fairly steady across recent seasons, though the exact boundaries can shift by a week or two depending on the schedule.

<div class="callout-legal">
<strong>Legal:</strong> The number of teams resting in a given week isn't evenly spread. Some weeks feature just two teams on a bye, while other weeks stagger four teams off at once, and one week, often called "byemageddon" by fantasy players, can see as many as six teams idle simultaneously. The league caps this at six teams off in any single week to keep the schedule from feeling thin.
</div>

## How the league actually decides bye week placement

Bye weeks aren't handed out by request, and teams don't get input on which week they land on. Instead, the NFL's schedule-makers determine bye placement based on a mix of factors including stadium availability, international and cross-country travel demands, and competitive fairness across the league.

![How Bye Week Scheduling Works in the NFL](/images/posts/nfl-bye-week-scheduling-2.webp)

A few specific factors drive the placement:

- **International games:** Teams that travel overseas for games in London, Germany, or elsewhere often have their bye placed in the following week to help them recover from the travel and time zone shift.
- **Stadium and venue conflicts:** If a team's home stadium is booked for another event, the league can use the bye week to work around that conflict.
- **Competitive balance:** The league tries to avoid giving any single team a scheduling advantage, such as stacking a bye right before or after a particularly tough division stretch, though this isn't guaranteed every year.
- **Thanksgiving and marquee weeks:** Weeks with heavy national broadcast importance, like Thanksgiving, typically have no byes at all so every team is in action.

## How the 2026 bye weeks break down

Bye weeks for the 2026 season fall between Week 5 and Week 14. Week 5 featured the Carolina Panthers and Kansas City Chiefs. Week 6 included the Cincinnati Bengals, Detroit Lions, Miami Dolphins, and Minnesota Vikings. Week 7 had the Buffalo Bills, Jacksonville Jaguars, Los Angeles Chargers, and Washington Commanders. Week 8 featured the Houston Texans, New Orleans Saints, New York Giants, and San Francisco 49ers. Week 9 had the Pittsburgh Steelers and Tennessee Titans. Week 10 included the Chicago Bears, Denver Broncos, Philadelphia Eagles, and Tampa Bay Buccaneers. Week 11 had six teams on bye: the Atlanta Falcons, Cleveland Browns, Green Bay Packers, Los Angeles Rams, New England Patriots, and Seattle Seahawks. Week 13 featured the Baltimore Ravens, Indianapolis Colts, Las Vegas Raiders, and New York Jets. Week 14 closed out the bye schedule with the Arizona Cardinals and Dallas Cowboys.

This is a useful example of how uneven the distribution can be. Some weeks have just two teams resting, while Week 11 alone accounted for six.

## Why bye weeks move every year

A team's bye week is not fixed from season to season. A team that had its bye in Week 5 one year could have it in Week 8 the next, since the entire schedule, including bye placement, is rebuilt from scratch every offseason. It has to account for a new set of opponents, home and away splits, prime-time game selections, and international game assignments each year, and bye weeks fall out of that larger calculation rather than being set independently.

## What this means for fantasy football managers

Bye weeks are one of the more disruptive parts of a fantasy football season because a player's real-life team having a bye means that player is unavailable for that week, regardless of form or matchup.

![How Bye Week Scheduling Works in the NFL](/images/posts/nfl-bye-week-scheduling-3.webp)

A few practical approaches fantasy managers use to handle this:

- **Spread your roster across bye weeks:** Avoid drafting too many starters who share the same bye week, particularly for weeks known to have heavy bye concentration like Week 11 in the 2026 slate.
- **Draft backups with different byes:** After locking in starters, prioritize handcuffs and depth pieces whose bye weeks don't overlap with your core players.
- **Check the schedule before trading or streaming:** Before making a trade or picking up a streaming option, check whether that player's bye week lines up with a playoff week in your league.
- **Don't overreact in the draft:** Reaching for a worse player purely to avoid a bye week conflict is generally not worth the drop in talent. Bye weeks are a one-week problem; talent gaps last all season.

<strong>Common mistake:</strong> Assuming last year's bye weeks will repeat is unreliable. Placement is rebuilt from scratch every offseason, so always check the current season's official schedule.
</div>

## Do other leagues have bye weeks

The NFL is the most prominent example, but the concept shows up elsewhere with different mechanics. College football (FBS) teams also get bye weeks, though scheduling is less centralized than the NFL's, since it's built around conference schedules rather than a single league office. The NBA and NHL don't use bye weeks in the traditional sense, since their much longer schedules are built around back-to-backs and rest days rather than a single dedicated week off for each team.
